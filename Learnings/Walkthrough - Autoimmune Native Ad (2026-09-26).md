# Walkthrough — the Autoimmune Native Ad

The first "recent piece of work" the CEO walked through. The ad itself: [[Winners - Ad Library]] W-001.

## What happened, as told

1. **Trigger:** the team had started testing long native storytelling ads and wanted more.
2. **Source:** István pulled English-language storytelling ads into the GetHookd swipe file.
3. **Adaptation:** Mercédesz and István swiped one and **translated it from English to Hungarian**, fitting the brand's book, the *kódolás* technique and the offer into the source's structure. The narrator (56, three years of stiffness, the doubting husband) came with the source.
4. **Visual:** a period-look B&W family photo in a field, matching the "when your grandmother was your age" open.
5. **Launch:** István set up the tests **from Lajos Antal's verified Facebook page**, pointing to a dedicated "fatigue" advertorial page.
6. **Scale:** it "did really well," and Mercédesz took over scaling.

## What the story reveals

- **The verified page is the scaling engine.** Ads from Antal's page survive longest and scale furthest; the name and badge do the trust work. Whitelisted pages work for storytelling too, but less. Implication: anything the brain writes for scale should be written in a voice that can sit on Antal's page.
- **Swipe → translate → test is the fastest lane the team has.** It skipped the "random ideation" bottleneck the CEO described on 2026-09-25 because the structure came ready-made. The brain's Monday swipe-file report should feed exactly this lane: full-structure long-form ads, not just hooks.
- **The winning format is the older-audience format.** ~1,100 words, a plain narrator in her fifties, a slow reveal, an expert's name, big offline-world imagery. That's [[Advertising to Older Audiences]] to the letter, and it matches the 50+/65+ buyer the CEO described.
- **The team's word for the method is *kódolás*.** The transcript's "coding cage" is resolved.
- **The CEO calls storytelling "bottom-of-funnel."** The copy reads cold and problem-aware. Either it's running on warm audiences and would work colder, or "BOF" here means "converts, not just engages." Worth pinning down because it changes where the next ten go.
- **Two products of the process are now brain assets:** the ad's anatomy (reusable spine) and the source-ad pattern (find more like it).

## The decision it forces

The watchlist the brain holds as a wall ([[Compliance and Claims Watchlist]]) forbids condition pairing, symptom-as-result lists and composite testimonials. **W-001 does all three and is the brand's best-scaling ad.** Both facts are true. The brain can't hold the wall and copy the winner at the same time, so Mercédesz decides:

- **Option A: the wall stands.** W-001 is a deliberate exception she owns. The brain writes new storytelling ads on the same spine with the condition names, symptom timelines and "I'm 56" swapped for claims-safe equivalents (calmer, more energy, "the stiffness I'd accepted," a real [R] reader's words with consent). Expect some loss of punch; test it against W-001.
- **Option B: practice sets the wall.** Founder-page advertorials in the health lane are policy. The brain inserts a **Brain override** in the watchlist saying so, keeps the hard floor (no "cure," no guarantee, no doctor-bashing, no fake prices, sources on every number) and writes more W-001s.

Until she picks, the brain will write in Option A style and flag where Option B would hit harder.

## The decision, made (2026-09-26, same day)

**Option B.** Mercédesz: "you can override the brain because this is still in the acceptable zone… but keep the hard floor: no cure, no guarantee, no doctor-bashing, no fake prices, sourced numbers. You can still write more like this." The override is at the top of [[Compliance and Claims Watchlist]]. She also corrected the ROAS targets: scale at 2.5, 2.0–2.2 acceptable, break-even 1.7 ([[Team and Operations]]).
