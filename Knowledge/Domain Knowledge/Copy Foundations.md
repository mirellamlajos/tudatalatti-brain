# Copy Foundations

**RULE:** If you write, rewrite, translate, adapt or grade any copy in this brain, you must work from this doc and the five foundation docs, show the Foundation Brief with the output, and end your message with "This copy is built on the Copy Foundations."

> **Brain rule — this is the bread and butter (Mirella, 2026-09-30).** "If copy is not based on these resources, then it's not good." Every hook, headline, static, script, native ad, advertorial, landing page, email and comment reply starts here. Copy that skips the Foundation Brief or fails the audit doesn't ship, however good it sounds.

## The five foundation docs

Read all five before any copy work. They're short on purpose.

| Doc | What it decides | From |
|---|---|---|
| [[Market Awareness and Sophistication]] | Which desire leads, where the buyer is, what the opening move is | Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*, part 1 |
| [[One Belief and the Ten Questions]] | The one thing the piece must make the reader believe, and the order of the sections | Albuquerque, *The 16-Word Sales Letter* |
| [[Belief Building in Long Copy]] | How the body builds desire, identity and belief, step by accepted step | Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*, part 2 |
| [[Persuasion Principles and Copy Checklist]] | Whether it ties to a real want, reads easily, proves itself, and is easy to act on | Whitman, *Cashvertising* |
| [[Testing Doctrine and Specificity]] | Whether every line is specific and true, and how the piece gets judged once live | Hopkins, *Scientific Advertising* |

Source digests with page references: `Research/Sources/Books/`. The brand's filled-in versions (one-belief sentences per offer, the stage read, the desire scores): [[Copy Foundations - Brand Application]] in `Strategy/`.

**Where this sits in the load order.** Brand context first (the five Brand docs in `CLAUDE.md`), then this doc and the five foundations, then the format doc for the job (`Hooks.md`, `Scriptwriting.md`, `Problem-Solution Headline Writer.md`, `Static Ad Recreation.md`, the native-ads skill, and so on). The format docs say *what shape*. The foundations say *what to say, to whom, in what order, and whether it's believable.*

## Step 1: the Foundation Brief (before the first draft)

Fill this in from the brand context and the data. Show it with the output. (A skill that writes an audit file, like `native-ads-creator`, puts the brief there instead of in the chat.)

```
FOUNDATION BRIEF
1. Desire that leads:      [one] — urgency / staying power / scope
2. Awareness state:        [unaware / problem / solution / product / most] — evidence
3. Sophistication stage:   [1–5] — evidence
4. Opening move:           [the move for that state and stage; pattern or headline move #]
5. One belief:             [new opportunity] is the key to [desire], only through [mechanism]
6. Questions carried:      [which of the ten this format carries, in order]
7. Belief chain:           [the hardest idea in the piece, and what prepares it]
8. Proof on file:          [each proof with its tag: F / R / Verified / Stated + source]
9. Want tapped:            [Life-Force 8 or learned want] → last rung of the benefit ladder
10. Specifics on file:     [the real numbers, steps and names this piece will use]
```

Short formats (headline, static, hook) fill lines 1–5 and 8. Everything longer fills all ten.

If a line can't be filled, tag it Data-limited, say what would settle it, and pick the most defensible option. If line 5's first slot can't be filled with something this market hasn't heard a hundred times, **stop and find a better idea before writing.**

## Step 2: what each format must carry

| Format | Minimum |
|---|---|
| **Headline, static, hook** | The opening move for the state and stage. One desire. One of the 38 moves, wall-tagged. Nothing clever that needs solving |
| **Short video script** | The opening move; questions 1 and 2; one proof with a "but" or one beat of the real problem; one scene the viewer can see; an easy call to action |
| **Native storytelling ad** | An identification opening (unaware patterns); questions 1–5, 7 and 8 inside the story; a belief chain with the mechanism arriving prepared; at least four intensification settings; one sensory scene per act; a sceptic or witness; a low-pressure close |
| **Advertorial, VSL, landing page** | All ten questions in order, 1–7 in the first third; proof at the moments the reader asks for it; the objection-first block; who it isn't for; the three-options close |
| **Email** | Questions 1 and 2, one proof, the offer, the close. The opening continues the argument that brought the reader onto the list |
| **Comment reply, FAQ answer** | The doubt stated fairly, then answered. Specific. No pressure |

## Step 3: the four-part audit (before anything ships)

Run all four on every draft. For short formats, parts 1 and 4 plus the specificity pass.

1. **Market fit** ([[Market Awareness and Sophistication]]). Does the opening match the state and the stage on the brief? For a cold audience: no price, no product name, no direct promise in the opening.
2. **Ten questions** ([[One Belief and the Ten Questions]]). Read it as the buyer. Is each question the format carries answered, in order? Does every section serve the one belief?
3. **Belief chain** ([[Belief Building in Long Copy]]). One line per block: what does it ask the reader to accept, and what prepared it? Fix every idea that arrives unprepared.
4. **Specificity and craft** ([[Testing Doctrine and Specificity]], [[Persuasion Principles and Copy Checklist]]). Run the specificity pass. Run the pre-ship checklist.

Then the existing lints, unchanged: `AI Writing Tells.md` on all written copy, `Spoken-Script Voice.md` on scripts, and the checklist in `Brand/Compliance and Claims Watchlist.md` §6.

**A failed audit means a rewrite, not a note.** Fix it, run the audit again, then deliver.

## When sources disagree, this order wins

1. **The compliance wall** (`Brand/Compliance and Claims Watchlist.md`, including the CEO's rulings in it). It outranks every book.
2. **The Andromeda rule** on iterations and delivery.
3. **Brain overrides** already on file (no fake prices, ROAS targets, trimming rules, localization rules in `Tribal Knowledge.md`).
4. **The five foundation docs.**
5. **The older format docs.** Where a format doc and a foundation doc disagree on strategy (what to say, which state, which stage), the foundation doc wins. Where they disagree on format mechanics (length limits, layout, platform rules), the format doc wins.

Between the foundation docs themselves: Schwartz sets the opening, Albuquerque sets the order, Schwartz sets the body craft, Whitman and Hopkins check the result. Hopkins's "show the bright side" against the failed-solution openings the brand's data favours is an open test, not a rule.

## What the books are not used for

- **Their examples.** Most are health, weight-loss, finance or patent-medicine ads with second-person hooks, invented numbers and false deadlines. The structure is kept; no example is swiped.
- **Their numbers.** Every figure in them is the author's claim [Stated]. None is quoted as a fact in an ad.
- ***The Game of Life*** (Shinn) is category research on what the Believer reader already believes. It is never a method source and never a source of claims.
- ***Profit First*** is parked. It isn't a copy resource.

## Grading someone else's copy

Same steps in reverse. Rebuild the Foundation Brief the copy seems to be working from, say where it breaks (wrong state, wrong stage, a missing question, an unprepared idea, a vague line), and give the fix. Name the foundation doc the fix comes from.
