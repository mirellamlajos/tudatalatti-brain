## Corpus profile

- **Records: ≈75.** Facebook: 15 unique comments (R01–R15), ≈ 2025-10-16 to 2026-03-19, hand-picked screenshots, dates ± a week, reactions recorded. Website: ~60 legible widget reviews, undated; the corpus doc records 26 with verbatim text (W01–W26) plus 4 unnumbered records mentioned only in its governors (the "M." review, a relative's illness, and the health clauses of W20/W23). **Only 41 records are readable verbatim in the brain.** Counts below stay "x of 75" so they line up with the 2026-09-24 reads; any count I derived fresh is really x of 41 readable, so the true rate across the 75 can only be equal or higher.
- **Selection:** the FB set was picked by the user; the widget showed 66 of 67 at 5★ when captured. **Both are positive-selected.** They can't measure satisfaction. They can give language, themes, and past-tense objections.
- **Languages:** Hungarian throughout; buyer flags DE, AT, SK on the widget (diaspora confirmed).
- **Read this as:** directional. Every objection in hunt 1 is one a buyer already got past, so it's a list of the brakes the next buyer still has, not a list of live pushback. Every "complaint" in hunt 4 is a soft one inside a 5★ review.
- **Missing:** negative and neutral reviews; retailer reviews (Moly.hu, Libri, Bookline); ad comments (the Monday report in [[Operating Cadence and AI Workflows]] isn't running yet, no Page access); course and event feedback; support tickets; the team's collected-reviews document and the "Magyar olvasói történetek" PDF ([[Stories and Proof Assets]]); the 5 replies under the founder's answer to R06 (the only live practice Q&A visible in the corpus, and it wasn't captured).

## 2. Comment-response hook candidates

### H1. Ezer módszer (the "tried everything" confession)
- **Comment:** „Ezer féle módszert végeztem de még mindig úgy érzem hogy valami vissza tart, blokkol valami ott bent." (R10, ≈2025-12-25, Facebook) [R]
- **Why it stops the scroll:** it's the 10-of-75 buyer story said out loud, by a real person, with a number. Investment hooks work hardest on 40+ and women-skewed audiences, and this one arrives as a comment, not a claim.
- **Response opener (HU), founder:** „Ezer módszer, és mindegyik a tudatos fejednek beszélt. Az meg nem dönt semmiről. Ami blokkol, az lentebb ül, és oda nem több akarat kell, hanem kevesebb. Aki ezer módszert eladott neked, egyszer sem szólt a tudatalattiról." (A thousand methods, and every one of them talked to your conscious mind. That part doesn't decide anything. - **Pairs with:** #1 + #15 myth-busting (the methods worked on the wrong part); #1 + #2 investment if a creator answers with their own count.
- **Compliance:** no second-person attribute (the "te" is the commenter, and it asserts nothing about her); no health outcome; the punch lands on "whoever sold you," not on the viewer: pass.

## 5. Headline candidates for statics

**Product:** Book 1, *A tudatalattid határtalan ereje – a tudatalatti kontroll*. **ICP:** the 50+ book buyer (most 65+, many retired; the 2026-09-25 correction in [[Interpreted Data]]), the one whose story is "tried many methods and nothing stuck." **Sophistication:** high. 10 of 75 name three or more failed methods (Silva, hypnosis, meditation, theology, "a thousand methods," "so many self-help books"), so the headlines acknowledge the burn rather than educate the problem. Boomer register: clarity, trust, social proof, no hype, no emotional language for its own sake.

**Problems ranked (one per headline):** (1) tried everything, nothing stuck · (2) everyday worries, can't calm down · (3) something inside holds me back · (4) I thought the fault was me · (5) self-help book fatigue. The high scorers are 1 and 2 because they're daily and they've beaten every fix; 3 and 4 carry the identity layer ("I'm my own obstacle," "I'm broken"); 5 is the shelf of wasted money.

**Customer language used:** „Ezer féle módszert végeztem" (R10) [R] · „valami vissza tart, blokkol valami ott bent" (R10) [R] · „Több, mint 20 év keresgélés után" (W02) [R] · „minden próbálkozás hiábavaló volt" (W03) [R] · „miért vettem annyi önfejlesztő könyvet fölöslegesen" (W05) [R] · „én azt hittem velem van a baj" (W06) [R] · „Bevallom, szkeptikus voltam" (W07) [R] · „végre valaki jól látja a dolgokat" (W07) [R], echo-flagged · „sokkal nyugodtabb vagyok" (W03, W10, W11) [R], three independent uses · „nem aggasztanak a mindennapi gondok úgy, mint azelőtt" (W03; R01 variant) [R] · „mind ott vannak, csak valahol lent elbújva bennem" (R05) [R] · „olyan mintha valami visszatartana" (W10) [R] · „Rengeteg könyvet olvastam, de ez ébresztett fel" (W16) [R] · „megtaláltam annak okát, hogy eddig miért nem működött" (W01) [R] · „Mindent jól csináltok, csak amit csináltok, az nem jó" [F]. *Nyugodtabb* is the outcome word.

**Walls applied:** Compliance §1–§5 as written (the storytelling override doesn't reach statics, §7). No cure or heal, no condition, no second-person attribute question, no unsourced number, no doctor-bashing, no *gyógyulás*. Hungarian, ten words or fewer, none presented as a quote (so no consent needed; the phrasing is borrowed, not attributed).

**5 HEADLINE OPTIONS FOR BOOK 1:**

**Option 1:** „Ezerféle módszert végeztem. Valami mégis blokkolt ott bent." (I've done a thousand kinds of methods. Something still blocked me in there.)
- **Problem Addressed:** tried everything, nothing stuck (with the "something inside" layer)
- **Sophistication Level:** high. It opens on the failed solutions, which is the only way a burned buyer keeps reading.
- **Customer Language Used:** „Ezer féle módszert végeztem… blokkol valami ott bent" (R10)
- **Why This Works:** it's the investment structure (the count of what was tried) in the first person, so nobody is told they have a problem; a reader who's lived it recognises her own sentence. The second half opens the gap the book closes ("blocked by what?"), and the mechanism answer sits on the card's body line, not the headline. The number is the customer's, so it reads lived, not written.

**Option 2:** „Miért nem működött eddig egyetlen önfejlesztő könyv sem?" (Why hasn't a single self-help book worked until now?)
- **Problem Addressed:** self-help book fatigue
- **Sophistication Level:** high. It assumes the shelf exists and asks the question the reader has already asked.
- **Customer Language Used:** „miért vettem annyi önfejlesztő könyvet fölöslegesen" (W05) · „Eddig nem értettem hogy bármilyen könyvet elolvastam… miért nem hatásos" (W04)
- **Why This Works:** a real question hook aimed at the reader's own history, not at a trait, so it clears the personal-attributes rule while still selecting the right person. Two independent reviewers say the book's value was exactly this answer ("finally logically explained why"), so the promise is one the product keeps. It also sells a book with a headline about books, which passes the no-brand-knowledge test.

**Option 3:** „Sokkal nyugodtabb vagyok, és nem kellett hozzá akaraterő." (I'm much calmer, and it didn't take willpower.)
- **Problem Addressed:** everyday worries, can't calm down
- **Sophistication Level:** high. The second clause names the failed tool (willpower) so the burned reader knows this isn't a discipline pitch.
- **Customer Language Used:** „sokkal nyugodtabb vagyok" (W03, W10, W11)
- **Why This Works:** a direct outcome promise in the brand's only claims-safe outcome word, said as a first-person result rather than as a claim about the viewer. "Akaraterő" appears only under attack, which is the founder's rule. It's the plainest of the five and the one the 65+ reader can repeat to a friend.

**Option 4:** „Nem hiányzott belőlem semmi. Csak elbújt, valahol lent." (Nothing was missing from me. It had just hidden, somewhere down below.)
- **Problem Addressed:** something inside holds me back (identity layer: "I'm my own obstacle")
- **Sophistication Level:** high. No education, just the customer's own picture of the mechanism.
- **Customer Language Used:** „mind ott vannak, csak valahol lent el bújva bennem" (R05, the 103-reaction comment)
- **Why This Works:** it's the metaphor structure, and the metaphor is a reader's, not ours; it makes the invisible mechanism (a program sitting below the conscious mind) into one image. It's the least self-explanatory of the five, so it wants the book mockup and a body line under it, or a native placement that sells after the click. Its resonance signal is the strongest in the corpus.

**Option 5 (breaks the brand's usual shape):** „Nem velem volt a baj. A módszerekkel." (The problem wasn't me. It was the methods.)
- **Problem Addressed:** I thought the fault was me
- **Sophistication Level:** high. Blame removal is the conversion move for people who've been burned three times and started blaming themselves.
- **Customer Language Used:** „én azt hittem velem van a baj" (W06) · the founder's „Mindent jól csináltok, csak amit csináltok, az nem jó" [F]
- **Why This Works:** the account's proven shapes are a myth-bust ("A vonzás törvénye nem működik") and the creed ("A változás belül kezdődik"). This is neither: a bold first-person declarative built on a customer confession, with the enemy named in the second sentence. Two beats, seven words, and the reader who has spent years thinking she's the broken part gets released in the headline itself. If the myth line is already on the mockup, this is the one that doesn't repeat it.

**Shape alternative held back:** „A könyv, amit az olvasói kötelezővé tennének." (The book its readers would make required reading), a social-proof structure on three independent uses (W19, W20, R03). Not in the five because it says nothing about the problem; it's a warm-audience line.

**MY RECOMMENDATION FOR WHAT TO TEST FIRST:** Option 1 and Option 5. Option 1 carries the highest-scoring problem on the strongest [R] language in the corpus, in the investment shape that works hardest on 40+ audiences. Option 5 is the shape-breaker, and blame removal is the move this high-sophistication buyer hasn't been shown yet; if it beats the myth family, the whole static library gets a new lane. Run Option 3 third as the outcome-word control.

**Brand Context Applied:**
- **What I used:** the 50+ / 65+ book buyer from the 2026-09-25 correction and its "tried many methods, nothing stuck" story; 15 exact [R] phrases from the two corpora with IDs, led by R10, W05, W06, R05 and the three-use *nyugodtabb* descriptor; the founder's blame-removal line [F]; the account's proven headline shapes from the Winners library and Interpreted Data, so one option deliberately breaks them; the Q4 bundle and Book 2 plateau as the calendar context.
- **What I avoided:** every health outcome in the corpus (R14, R15's medication clause, R11's pain, W10's phobia, W17's "medicine," W20/W23's healing lines); "gyógyulás" and condition names; second-person attribute questions (all five headlines are first person or impersonal); unsourced numbers (no 90%, 97% or 95%; "ezer" and "20 év" are customers' own words); the hype words and *akaraterő / kitartás / komfortzóna* in a positive sense; presenting any [R] line as a quote, so no headline needs consent; a composite dressed as a comment (H8 is marked direction only).
- **Why this fits:** the brand's brake is scepticism after decades of failed methods, and every headline opens on that burn instead of educating the problem. The account is scaling on native storytelling and a myth-headline static family; these five give the Monday build a Book 1 static lane that uses real reader words, plus one shape it hasn't run. With the Q4 bundle (Book 1 + Book 2 + Guide) coming, Option 5 and the H3 practice hook also set up the Book 2 plateau story for the warm audience.
